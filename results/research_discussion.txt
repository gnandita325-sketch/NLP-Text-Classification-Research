DISCUSSION ON COMPARATIVE RESEARCH

1.Comparison of model performance

The experimental evaluation revealed that Linear Support Vector Machine (Linear SVM) performed the best overall among the three classifiers that were evaluated. The results of five-fold stratified cross-validation showed that Linear SVM achieved a mean accuracy of 98.69% and a macro F1-score of 96.95%.

Multinomial Naive Bayes obtained a mean accuracy of 96.74% and macro F1-score of 91.60%, while Logistic Regression obtained 96.47% accuracy and macro F1-score of 90.84%.

2.Results Interpretation

The good performance of Linear SVM could be due to its margin based learning, which is effective for high dimensional text representations such as TF-IDF features.

Multinomial Naive Bayes is a probabilistic text classification approach. Logistic Regression is a linear decision boundary learned through supervised optimization. The differences in performance seen may be attributed to their different learning assumptions.

3. Cross-Validation Results

The linear SVM produced an accuracy standard deviation around 0.23 percentage points for the five folds, suggesting the model’s performance was fairly stable across the training partitions tested.

This provides further evidence for its performance beyond a single train-test split.

4.Analysis of errors

The linear SVM attained an accuracy of 98.16 % and spam F1-score of 92.24 % on the independent test set.

The model was able to correctly classify 113 out of 131 spam messages while 18 spam messages were classified as legitimate. One legitimate message was falsely classified as spam.

Among the misclassified examples were short messages, promotional messages and event announcements. These examples indicate possible problems with vocabulary diversity and scarce context information.

5. Study Limitations

The evaluation is performed on a single SMS spam dataset.

The dataset is imbalanced with more legit messages than spam messages.

Cross-validation was performed on the training subset.

Final test evaluation is done on one held-out split.

The computational times are measured over single runs and may vary.

The study does not establish performance on unseen real-world data.

Conclusion

Among the three evaluated machine learning algorithms, Linear SVM demonstrated the strongest overall classification performance. Its high macro F1-score and relatively low cross-validation accuracy variation make it a promising approach for SMS spam classification.

Further experiments using additional datasets and more extensive validation would help establish the generalizability of these findings.
